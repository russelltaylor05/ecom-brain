Content Studio Script Form
The data gathering document. Fill it out once, paste the whole thing into the tool.
Read this first
This document is the raw material. The scripts you get back are only as good as what you put in here, so treat it like an inventory count, not a form.
Paste, don't summarize. If a question asks for reviews, paste the reviews. If it asks how you talk, paste a transcript of you talking. The tool is going to write in your voice and about your customers, and the only way it can do that is if your voice and your customers are in this document, word for word.
Four rules
• Real numbers only. If you don't know a number, write UNKNOWN. Don't guess.
• Nothing in here gets published. Be blunt about what's failed.
• Skip nothing. A blank answer becomes a made-up answer, and you'll have to catch it later.
• Gray boxes are where you type. Make them as long as you need.
The five things that matter most
If you only have an hour, do these five. Everything else makes the scripts better. These five make them possible.
• Part 4, question 1. Twenty raw reviews, pasted.
• Part 8, question 1. A two-minute transcript of you talking, unscripted.
• Part 3. The product facts, and the numbers you will and will not say on camera.
• Part 2, questions 7 and 8. The one thing you're selling and where the link goes.
• Part 11. What's off limits.

For the writer
This page is not for the member.
Before writing a single script, check the intake for these five things. If any is missing, send it back.
1. At least 20 raw reviews (4.1). No reviews, no customer language, no scripts.
2. The two-minute transcript (8.1) or the five written answers (8.2). No voice sample, no voice.
3. The product facts sheet (3.1 to 3.6) with the numbers they will and won't say.
4. The one thing they sell (2.7) and where the CTA goes (2.8).
5. The off-limits list (Part 11).
Then write to the standard.
• Four scripts per awareness level. Unaware, Problem Aware, Solution Aware, Product Aware, Most Aware.
• Punchy hook, straight into the meat. No preamble. If the video could start at sentence three, sentence three is the hook.
• Simple, fluid sentences. No fragments. No taglines. The member supplies tone, cadence, and inflection. The words stay plain.
• Count every list. If it says three things, list three things.
• Real numbers only, from the intake. Flag anything not in the intake with [FACT].
• Never use a word from 3.8. Never touch anything in Part 11.
• Every script gets the type, the on-screen text hook, the spoken hook, the body, and the CTA.
